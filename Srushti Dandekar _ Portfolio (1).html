City-scale retrofit analysis of Ahmedabad with CityBES

Retrofitting a city begins with knowing what the city is made of. I built that picture for Ahmedabad's housing stock and then asked which buildings are worth fixing first.

Building the dataset. Using OpenStreetMap and QGIS, I created a residential building dataset for the whole city, extracting footprints, floor area, height, coordinates and other geometric attributes that an energy model needs.

Giving the model a climate. I processed ERA5-Land reanalysis data in Google Earth Engine and fed the resulting weather inputs into CityBES, so simulations reflected Ahmedabad's real conditions.

Finding the biggest savings. I benchmarked buildings on Site and Source EUI, electricity use intensity, cooling energy, peak load and operational greenhouse gas emissions to flag the residential retrofits with the highest impact.

Geopolymer concrete as a sustainable alternative to cement

Cement is among the most carbon-intensive materials we build with. This study asked whether industrial waste can replace it.

Mechanisms. I investigated how cement hydrates and how geopolymerization works when fly ash, GGBS and red mud serve as binders.

Performance. I evaluated how supplementary cementitious materials change strength, durability and thermal behaviour, then compared Portland cement and geopolymer concrete side by side to judge which is better suited to sustainable construction.

Detecting waste dump yards from space

Illegal and unmanaged dump yards are hard to find on foot. I built an end-to-end pipeline that finds them automatically from Sentinel-2 imagery.

Data. I assembled a multi-region dataset with OpenStreetMap and Overpass Turbo, handling boundary extraction, image retrieval, masking and spectral feature engineering (including NDVI and NDBI).

Models. I benchmarked Random Forest, CNN, ResNet and a hybrid of CNN and MLP. Combining spatial and spectral features gave the best result, reaching 98% classification accuracy.

Forest fire-prone areas in Uttarakhand

Where do forests burn, and why there? I mapped the state's fire history and built models to predict where fires are most likely next.

Mapping. With MODIS data, Google Earth Engine and QGIS, I traced long-term patterns in where fires occur, how far they spread and how intense they get.

Drivers and prediction. I analysed the environmental factors behind fire occurrence and spread, then trained machine learning models that classify fire-prone zones, supporting early warning and prevention.

How much can a bridge made of spaghetti carry?

A hands-on structural engineering challenge: design a Howe truss bridge from spaghetti, predict how it will behave, then break it on purpose to find out if the prediction was right.

Design and build. I designed and fabricated the Howe truss bridge to mimic how a real structure carries load.

Predict. Hand calculations and a SAP2000 model gave the expected member forces and deflections.

Test. On a universal testing machine (UTM), the bridge reached a load-to-weight ratio of 4.25, and the results were validated against the simulations.
